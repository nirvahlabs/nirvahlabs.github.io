MMGS-II to MMGS-III promotion exam: an 8-week preparation plan

The written exam is on 26 December 2026. Here is a week-by-week plan for a working officer who can give 45-60 minutes a day, built on the official pattern: 140 objective questions in 100 minutes, plus a typed descriptive paper of 30 marks.

Know the paper first

Objective paper: 140 questions × 0.5 = 70 marks, 100 minutes (about 43 seconds a question). Descriptive paper: 30 marks in 80 minutes, typed. Total 100 marks.

The 8-week plan

Weeks 1-2 · Biggest sections first

  • Start with Systems and procedures, organisation structure, HR (20 questions), then the four 15-question sections: Product features: deposit / liability, Product features: advances / assets, Government business, cross-selling, financial inclusion, Laws relevant to banks.
  • Every day: 20 questions from one section, and read every explanation, right or wrong.
  • Start a one-page list of figures: limits, ages, days, rates and Act sections.
  • Every second day: one short descriptive answer (rationale or for-and-against).

Weeks 3-4 · Cover everything once

  • Finish the remaining sections: digital channels, macro-economy, supervisory role, forex and data interpretation.
  • End of week 3: your first full mock in exam conditions. Note your two weakest sections.
  • Daily: clear yesterday's mistakes before starting new questions.

Weeks 5-6 · Strengthen

  • One full mock every week; spend the next days on the two weakest sections.
  • One complete descriptive paper per week, timed at 80 minutes.
  • Revise your figures list twice a week; check the recent rule changes.

Weeks 7-8 · Mocks and revision

  • Two mocks a week, each followed by a review of every wrong answer.
  • No new material in the last 5 days: mistakes, figures and recent changes only.
  • Day before: a short revision of figures, then rest. See the last-30-days plan.

Frequently asked

How many hours a day do I need for the MMGS-III exam?

45-60 minutes on most days is enough if you are regular for 8 weeks. Consistency matters more than long weekend sessions.

Which sections should I study first?

The sections with the most marks: Systems and procedures, organisation structure, HR (20 questions), then deposits, advances, government business, laws and NPA management (15 questions each).

How many mocks should I take?

At least 6-8 full mocks: one a week from week 3, and two a week in the last two weeks, each followed by a review of every wrong answer.

Can I write the descriptive paper in Hindi?

Rationale, For and against and Situational analysis may be written in English or Hindi; Comprehension and Business correspondence only in English.
