Product features: deposits and loans

1. Under the DICGC deposit insurance scheme, a depositor's deposits in one insured bank, held in the same right and capacity, are covered up to:

  1. ₹1 lakh
  2. ₹2 lakh
  3. ₹5 lakh
  4. ₹10 lakh
  5. ₹5 lakh per account

Answer: C. ₹5 lakh

The cover is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (not per account or per branch), for principal and interest together, across all accounts held in the same right and capacity. It was raised from ₹1 lakh with effect from 4 February 2020.

Source: DICGC Act, 1961; DICGC cover revision effective 4 Feb 2020 Check the source ↗

2. A savings or current account is treated as inoperative when there has been no customer-induced transaction for more than:

  1. 1 year
  2. 2 years
  3. 3 years
  4. Over 2 years, counting interest credits as transactions
  5. 18 months

Answer: B. 2 years

An account with no customer-induced transaction for over two years is classified as inoperative. Interest credited by the bank does not count as a customer-induced transaction.

Source: RBI circular on Inoperative Accounts and Unclaimed Deposits, 1 Jan 2024 Check the source ↗

3. RBI deregulated the interest rate on savings bank deposits with effect from:

  1. 1 April 2010
  2. 1 April 2011
  3. 25 October 2011
  4. 1 April 2016
  5. 16 December 2019

Answer: C. 25 October 2011

Banks have been free to set their savings bank rate since 25 October 2011. Conditions: a Board-approved comprehensive policy on deposit rates; a uniform rate for balances up to ₹1 lakh; and above ₹1 lakh banks may give differential rates, but must not discriminate between similar deposits of the same size accepted on the same date at any of their offices.

Source: RBI Master Direction - Interest Rate on Deposits, 2016 (as amended) Check the source ↗

4. Interest earned on an NRE account is:

  1. Taxable at the normal slab rates
  2. Taxed at a flat 30%
  3. Exempt from income tax in India
  4. Taxable only above ₹50,000 a year
  5. Exempt only if the depositor is a resident

Answer: C. Exempt from income tax in India

Interest on NRE balances is exempt from income tax in India (Income-tax Act, 2025, Schedule IV, item 1; formerly Sec 10(4)(ii) of the 1961 Act) for an individual who is a person resident outside India under FEMA, or who is permitted by RBI to maintain the account. When the holder returns and becomes resident, RBI requires the NRE account to be redesignated as a resident account (or the funds moved to an RFC account), and the interest thereafter is taxable.

Source: Income-tax Act, 2025 (exemption formerly Sec 10(4)(ii) of the 1961 Act) Check the source ↗

5. In a joint term deposit with the mandate "Former or Survivor", who can withdraw the matured deposit while both holders are alive?

  1. Either holder
  2. Both holders jointly
  3. Only the first-named holder
  4. Only the second-named holder
  5. The first-named holder, or the second with a written mandate from the first

Answer: C. Only the first-named holder

Under "Former or Survivor", only the first-named holder can operate/withdraw the deposit on maturity while both are alive; premature withdrawal may need the signature of both. On the death of the former, the survivor receives the proceeds; payment to the survivor gives the bank a valid discharge, and the survivor holds the money as trustee for the legal heirs of the deceased.

Source: RBI (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025 (joint deposits / settlement of claims of deceased depositors) Check the source ↗

6. Before DICGC's Risk-Based Premium Framework took effect on 1 April 2026, the flat deposit insurance premium paid by every insured bank was:

  1. 5 paise per ₹100 of assessable deposits
  2. 15 paise per ₹100
  3. 10 paise per ₹100
  4. 12 paise per ₹100
  5. 25 paise per ₹100

Answer: D. 12 paise per ₹100

From 1 April 2020 the flat premium was 12 paise per ₹100 of assessable deposits (raised from 10 paise). From 1 April 2026 DICGC moved to a Risk-Based Premium Framework: 12 paise is the card rate, and better-rated banks pay less (rates vary by bank, with a vintage adjustment). The premium is borne by the bank and cannot be passed on to depositors.

Source: DICGC Act, 1961; DICGC Risk-Based Premium Framework; DICGC Annual Report 2025-26 Check the source ↗

7. The rate of interest on a recurring deposit is generally:

  1. The savings bank rate
  2. The term deposit rate applicable for the same tenor
  3. Fixed by RBI for all banks
  4. The term deposit rate minus 1%
  5. Zero for the first year

Answer: B. The term deposit rate applicable for the same tenor

Recurring deposits are a form of term deposit, and banks usually pay the term deposit rate for the corresponding maturity.

Source: RBI Master Direction - Interest Rate on Deposits, 2016 (as amended) Check the source ↗

8. Under SBI's Savings Bank Rules, a minor of 10 years or more who can sign uniformly may open an account in his or her single name with a maximum balance of ____; if the minor opens a joint account with the guardian, the cap is ____.

  1. ₹5 lakh; ₹10 lakh
  2. ₹10 lakh; ₹20 lakh
  3. ₹10 lakh; no limit
  4. ₹2 lakh; ₹5 lakh
  5. ₹20 lakh; ₹50 lakh

Answer: B. ₹10 lakh; ₹20 lakh

The SB Rules (abridged) cap a minor's single account at ₹10 lakh and a joint account with the guardian at ₹20 lakh. Apart from these and the small account, there is no ceiling on SB balances.

11. A customer reports an unauthorised electronic debit within 3 working days of receiving the bank's communication about it, and the fraud was due to a third-party breach (neither the bank nor the customer at fault). The customer's liability is:

  1. Nil
  2. The transaction value or ₹5,000, whichever is lower
  3. Up to ₹5,000
  4. Up to ₹10,000
  5. The full amount

Answer: A. Nil

Zero liability: where the deficiency lies elsewhere in the system (third-party breach) and the customer reports within 3 working days of receiving the bank's communication (the day of receipt excluded). If reported in 4-7 working days, liability is limited to the transaction value or a fixed cap (₹5,000 to ₹25,000 depending on account type), whichever is lower.

Source: RBI circular on Customer Protection - Limiting Liability of Customers in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions, 6 Jul 2017 Check the source ↗

Laws, government schemes and NPA management

17. Under RBI's framework for early recognition of stress, an account with principal or interest overdue for 61 to 90 days is classified as:

  1. SMA-0
  2. SMA-1
  3. SMA-2
  4. Sub-standard asset
  5. NPA

Answer: C. SMA-2

SMA-0 is up to 30 days overdue, SMA-1 is 31 to 60 days, and SMA-2 is 61 to 90 days. Beyond 90 days the account becomes an NPA.

Source: RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets, 7 Jun 2019 Check the source ↗

18. Interest on a non-performing asset is recognised as income:

  1. When it falls due
  2. When it is debited to the account
  3. Only when it is actually realised
  4. On accrual, if security value covers the loan
  5. At the end of each quarter

Answer: C. Only when it is actually realised

Income from NPAs is recognised only on realisation, not on accrual.

Source: RBI Master Circular - Prudential norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning Check the source ↗

19. A bill of exchange payable after a stated period (for example, 90 days after sight) is a:

  1. Demand bill
  2. Usance bill
  3. Accommodation bill
  4. Documentary demand bill
  5. Clean bill

Answer: B. Usance bill

Usance bills are payable after a period and are financed by discounting; demand bills are payable on presentation and are financed by purchase.

Source: Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 Check the source ↗

20. Payment systems in India are regulated and supervised by RBI under:

  1. The Banking Regulation Act, 1949
  2. The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
  3. The Information Technology Act, 2000
  4. The RBI Act, 1934, Section 45L
  5. The FEMA, 1999

Answer: B. The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007

The Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 designates RBI as the authority to regulate and supervise payment systems. RBI exercises these powers through the Payments Regulatory Board (PRB), which replaced the Board for Regulation and Supervision of Payment and Settlement Systems (BPSS) from 9 May 2025.

Source: Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 Check the source ↗

21. Under the RTI Act, information concerning the life or liberty of a person must be supplied within:

  1. 24 hours
  2. 48 hours
  3. 72 hours

  5. 7 days

Answer: B. 48 hours

Life or liberty: 48 hours. Normal course: 30 days. When received through a CAPIO, 5 days are added to either limit. If the time limit is missed, the information must be given free of charge.

  5. No limit for permitted purposes

The LRS limit is USD 2,50,000 per financial year for permitted current and capital account transactions.

26. FEMA, which replaced FERA, came into force on:

  1. 1 April 1999
  2. 1 June 2000
  3. 1 January 2001
  4. 15 August 2000
  5. 29 December 1999

Answer: B. 1 June 2000

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 came into force on 1 June 2000. Unlike FERA, FEMA generally treats contraventions as civil matters with monetary penalties; the exception is Sec 13(1C), under which specified contraventions involving foreign assets can attract imprisonment up to 5 years.

Source: Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 Check the source ↗

27. When the forward rate of the US dollar against the rupee is higher than the spot rate, the dollar is said to be at a:

  1. Forward discount
  2. Forward premium
  3. Par
  4. Swap discount
  5. Cross rate

Answer: B. Forward premium

The forward premium usually reflects the interest rate difference between the two currencies.

31. Which remittance is NOT permitted under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme?

  1. Gift to a relative abroad
  2. Studies abroad
  3. Margin or margin calls to an overseas exchange
  4. Maintenance of a close relative abroad
  5. Purchase of property abroad

Answer: C. Margin or margin calls to an overseas exchange

Margin or margin calls to overseas exchanges are not allowed under LRS. Also barred: items prohibited under Schedule I or restricted under Schedule II of the Current Account Transaction Rules, and capital-account remittances to countries identified by FATF as non-cooperative. LRS is open to resident individuals including minors (Form A2 signed by the natural guardian).

Source: RBI Master Direction - Liberalised Remittance Scheme, upd. 6 Sep 2024 (rbi.org.in), read Oct 2026 Check the source ↗
