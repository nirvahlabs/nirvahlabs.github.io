Law Dictionary: which word is defined in which Act and section

Guarantee, pledge, lien, mortgage, holder in due course, beneficial owner: the legal words bank promotion exams ask about, each with its Act, section, a one-line meaning and a ready line to quote in your descriptive answer or interview. 31 key words are free below; the Unnati app has all 162, with search, filters by Act and a quiz.

Indian Contract Act, 1872

Contract · Sec 2(h), Indian Contract Act, 1872

An agreement enforceable by law.

In your answer: Every loan document is a contract, so it must satisfy Sec 10 of the Contract Act.

Consideration · Sec 2(d), Indian Contract Act, 1872

Something done, abstained from or promised at the desire of the promisor: the price of the promise.

In your answer: Without consideration an agreement is void (Sec 25), so loan documents record it clearly.

Valid contract · Sec 10, Indian Contract Act, 1872

Free consent of competent parties, lawful consideration and object, and not expressly declared void.

In your answer: The branch must ensure the borrower is competent and gives free consent (Secs 10-14) before execution.

Competent to contract · Sec 11, Indian Contract Act, 1872

A person who is a major, of sound mind and not disqualified by law.

In your answer: A minor's agreement is void, so loans are not given to minors in their own name.

Indemnity · Sec 124, Indian Contract Act, 1872

A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused by the promisor or any other person.

In your answer: Before issuing a duplicate draft, the bank takes an indemnity (Sec 124) from the purchaser.

Guarantee · Sec 126, Indian Contract Act, 1872

A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person if he defaults.

In your answer: A guarantee (Sec 126) gives the bank a second way out if the borrower defaults.

Surety, principal debtor, creditor · Sec 126, Indian Contract Act, 1872

Surety gives the guarantee; principal debtor is the person whose default is guaranteed; creditor is the person to whom it is given.

Continuing guarantee · Sec 129, Indian Contract Act, 1872

A guarantee that extends to a series of transactions.

In your answer: For running cash credit limits the bank takes a continuing guarantee (Sec 129).

Bailment · Sec 148, Indian Contract Act, 1872

Delivery of goods by one person to another for a purpose, to be returned or disposed of as directed when the purpose is over.

In your answer: Safe custody of sealed covers is a bailment (Sec 148): the bank must take reasonable care (Sec 151).

General lien · Sec 171, Indian Contract Act, 1872

Bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys and policy-brokers may retain goods bailed to them for a general balance of account.

In your answer: A banker's general lien (Sec 171) lets the bank retain securities in its possession for the customer's dues.

Pledge · Sec 172, Indian Contract Act, 1872

Bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise; pawnor pledges, pawnee receives.

In your answer: In a gold loan the gold is pledged (Sec 172), so the bank keeps possession till repayment.

Transfer of Property Act, 1882

Mortgage · Sec 58(a), Transfer of Property Act, 1882

Transfer of an interest in specific immovable property to secure a loan, an existing or future debt, or an engagement that may create a pecuniary liability.

In your answer: A home loan is secured by a mortgage (Sec 58, TPA) of the house.

Mortgagor, mortgagee, mortgage-money, mortgage-deed · Sec 58(a), Transfer of Property Act, 1882

Mortgagor transfers; mortgagee receives; mortgage-money is the principal and interest secured; mortgage-deed is the instrument.

Equitable mortgage (deposit of title deeds) · Sec 58(f), Transfer of Property Act, 1882

Delivery of title deeds with intent to create security, in Kolkata, Chennai, Mumbai and other notified towns.

In your answer: Most branch property loans use an equitable mortgage (Sec 58(f)) by deposit of title deeds.

Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

Promissory note · Sec 4, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

A written, signed, unconditional undertaking to pay a certain sum to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer.

In your answer: The demand promissory note is the basic loan document, made under Sec 4 of the NI Act.

Cheque · Sec 6, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

A bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable only on demand; includes truncated and electronic cheques.

Holder in due course · Sec 9, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

A holder who took the instrument for consideration, before maturity, and in good faith without cause to believe the title was defective.

Negotiable instrument · Sec 13, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

A promissory note, bill of exchange or cheque payable to order or to bearer.

Dishonour of cheque for insufficient funds · Sec 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

An offence: notice within 30 days of the return memo, 15 days to pay, complaint within one month after that.

In your answer: Sec 138 makes cheque bounce a criminal offence, which strengthens recovery.

Banking Regulation Act, 1949

Banking · Sec 5(b), Banking Regulation Act, 1949

Accepting deposits of money from the public for lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise.

In your answer: By Sec 5(b), deposits and lending together make a business "banking".

Depositor Education and Awareness Fund · Sec 26A, Banking Regulation Act, 1949

Deposits unclaimed for 10 years go to the DEA Fund; the depositor can still claim them from the bank.

In your answer: Unclaimed deposits move to the DEA Fund under Sec 26A, yet the customer never loses the right to claim.

Nomination for deposits · Sec 45ZA, Banking Regulation Act, 1949

A depositor may nominate up to four persons; on death the bank pays the nominee and gets a valid discharge.

In your answer: Nomination (Sec 45ZA) lets the branch settle deceased claims quickly without legal documents.

SARFAESI Act, 2002

Demand notice · Sec 13(2), SARFAESI Act, 2002

After the account becomes NPA, a notice asking the borrower to repay in full within 60 days.

In your answer: Recovery under SARFAESI starts with a 60-day demand notice under Sec 13(2).

Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

Financial creditor and financial debt · Secs 5(7), 5(8), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

A person to whom a financial debt (disbursed against the time value of money, e.g. a bank loan) is owed.

Moratorium · Sec 14, Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

On admission, suits, execution of decrees and enforcement of security (including SARFAESI) against the debtor are barred.

Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002

Beneficial owner · Sec 2(1)(fa), Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002

An individual who ultimately owns or controls a client, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted.

In your answer: KYC is incomplete until the beneficial owner (PMLA Sec 2(1)(fa)) of a company customer is identified.

Client · Sec 2(1)(ha), Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002

A person engaged in a financial transaction or activity with a reporting entity, including a person on whose behalf he acts.

Offence of money-laundering · Sec 3, Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002

Knowingly being involved in any process or activity connected with the proceeds of crime and projecting it as untainted.

In your answer: Under Sec 3 of the PMLA even helping to conceal proceeds of crime is money-laundering, so staff must report suspicion.

Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Consumer · Sec 2(7), Consumer Protection Act, 2019

A person who buys goods or hires services for consideration, but not for resale or commercial purpose.

In your answer: A bank customer is a consumer under Sec 2(7), so deficiency in service can reach a Consumer Commission.

Deficiency · Sec 2(11), Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature or manner of service.

Limitation Act, 1963

Acknowledgment of debt · Sec 18, Limitation Act, 1963

A written, signed acknowledgment before the period expires starts a fresh limitation period.

In your answer: A signed balance confirmation is an acknowledgment under Sec 18, so limitation starts afresh.
